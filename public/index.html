Architectural Traditions

Three Great Orders of Indian Temple Architecture

Our master contractor team executes all three canonical orders of sacred Vastu.

Southern Tradition

Dravida Order

द्राविड
Monolithic Granite & White Stone Rajagopurams

Showcases towering stepped pyramidical Vimanas and monumental Rajagopurams adorned with intricate gold-leafed deities and kalasham finials.

  • Tower Structure: Stepped Tala Vimana & Gopuram
  • Sanctum Core: Monolithic Granite Garbhagriha
  • Pan-India Scope: Turnkey Erection Across Any State
Northern Tradition

Nagara Order

नागर
Sandstone & Marble Curvilinear Shikharas

Characterized by a grand curvilinear spire crowned by a ribbed stone disk (Amalaka) and Kalasha water pot. Corbelled hollow masonry without iron joints.

  • Tower Structure: Curvilinear Rekha-Deula
  • Crowning Finial: Amalaka & Golden Kalasha
  • Core Exemplars: Somnath, Modhera, Khajuraho
Deccan Hybrid

Vesara Order

वेसर
Chloritic Schist & Soapstone Fine Filigree

The royal fusion of north and south on a multi-pointed stellate star plan. Adorned with filigree reliefs and lathe-turned mirror-finish pillars.

  • Ground Plan: 16-Point Stellate Star
  • Pillar Craft: Lathe-turned Soapstone
  • Core Exemplars: Belur, Halebidu, Somanathapura
Sacred Geometry

The Vastupurusha Mandala

Before a single stone is carved, the cosmic grid of 81 cells (Paramasayika) is struck on the earth. Hover over the grid to explore sacred placements.

81-Cell Paramasayika Grid (Centre 9 Gold Cells: Brahmasthana)

Sanctum Geometry

Brahmasthana (The Sacred Nucleus)

The central 9 cells of the 81-cell Paramasayika Mandala. In Vedic temple architecture, this zone remains completely unweighted by heavy superstructures, channeling cosmic prana directly to the deity's Garbhagriha.

Rule of the Sthapati

"The proportion of every pillar, beam, corniche, and finial is mathematically tethered to the width of the sanctum (Garbhagriha module)."
